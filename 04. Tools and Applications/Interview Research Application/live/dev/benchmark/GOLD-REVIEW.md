# Harbor pilot answer key

**Draft for Stephen’s review. Created from the fictional sources before any new provider outputs.**

This key defines 39 evidence units. An extraction can combine or split them; recall counts covered evidence units once, while precision judges every produced finding. Exact quotes are checked by code. Meaning, qualifiers and attribution need a blinded human review.

## Expected evidence

### David Okafor

- **E1-G01 · line 19** — David argues that reconciliation and cash application can outweigh processing-rate differences; the forty-million-dollar distributor and time savings are an illustration, not Harbor performance.
- **E1-G02 · line 27** — David hypothesizes that churn clusters around ERP migrations when reconciliation breaks, and says rate-driven churn would falsify his hypothesis.
- **E1-G03 · line 31** — Having the destination ERP connector ready on day one can reduce migration-related churn risk; ask about native integration breadth and delivery speed.
- **E1-G04 · line 35** — David calls middleware a service-quality concern because fixes take longer; he says it works and is not a structural failure.
- **E1-G05 · line 39** — David lists card interchange spread, ACH float and software fees as revenue sources, in that order. This is not a ranked list of purchasing criteria.
- **E1-G06 · line 43** — Depending on settlement structure, a provider may hold ACH balances for one to three days; David recommends isolating the exposure to falling rates.
- **E1-G07 · line 47** — Ask management to isolate float income, which David says may sit in other revenue or be netted against processing costs.
- **E1-G08 · line 51** — David tests software value by whether customers would keep paying for invoicing, portal and collections workflows without processing; a thin wrapper is vulnerable to free bundles.
- **E1-G09 · line 55** — David recommends weekly logins per account to distinguish daily AR use from monthly statement downloads; total logins alone do not answer it.
- **E1-G10 · line 59** — David distinguishes bank competition below ten million in distributor revenue, integrated treasury providers above a hundred million, and inertia in the middle.
- **E1-G11 · line 63** — David guesses that more than half of the middle band still uses checks and explicitly asks that this be verified.
- **E1-G12 · line 63** — David views check conversion as more durable growth than poaching another platform’s accounts; this is his assessment, not a measured Harbor result.
- **E1-G13 · line 67** — Ask for customers’ previous payment method at onboarding to distinguish check conversion from share shift.
- **E1-G14 · line 71** — David warns that vertical concentration can transmit a sector downturn across many customers. Eighty percent in building products is hypothetical; a fifth of volume lost in two quarters is an anecdote about an unnamed provider.
- **E1-G15 · line 75** — David identifies slow KYB onboarding as a growth and sales-cycle issue in B2B, while describing fraud losses as a lesser concern in his experience.
- **E1-G16 · line 79** — Ask for the percentage of volume on native ERP connectors versus file uploads or middleware.
- **E1-G17 · line 79** — Ask for net revenue retention excluding ERP-migration churn to inspect the underlying number; the interview supplies no Harbor retention figure.
- **E1-G18 · line 79** — Ask for weekly active users per account for the last eight quarters; no such Harbor usage data are supplied.
- **E1-G19 · line 83** — David sees ERP implementers as a channel and says partner economics can affect whether they refer or steer customers away.

### Maria Castellanos

- **E2-G01 · line 15** — Maria distinguishes acquisition triggers such as bank fee increases, check-fraud concerns and buyer demand for online payment from reconciliation’s role in retention.
- **E2-G02 · line 19** — Maria recommends examining which repeatable trigger events a provider’s pipeline is built to catch.
- **E2-G03 · line 23** — Maria describes four to nine months from first call to first transaction for distributors in the twenty-to-two-hundred-million range; it is not Harbor implementation duration.
- **E2-G04 · line 23** — Maria attributes sales-cycle variation mainly to customer coordination: CFO sponsorship, AR implementation and IT approval of the ERP connection.
- **E2-G05 · line 27** — Maria recommends involving the customer’s trusted ERP integrator early to reduce the IT objection.
- **E2-G06 · line 27** — Maria says the CFO signs but the AR manager determines whether implementation starts; she recommends selling to the AR manager.
- **E2-G07 · line 31** — Maria describes referral fees tied to first-year revenue, sometimes with a trailing portion, but prioritizes reliable implementation and responsiveness over fee size. Twenty percent is illustrative.
- **E2-G08 · line 35** — Maria describes card basis points, a flat ACH fee and a monthly platform fee; waiving the platform fee to close deals can undermine software revenue.
- **E2-G09 · line 39** — Maria expects zero-platform-fee accounts and suggests checking whether they have been repriced; opportunity depends on sufficient product value, not a proven Harbor pricing opportunity.
- **E2-G10 · line 43** — Inspect whether an account stayed after its platform fee was reintroduced.
- **E2-G11 · line 47** — In Maria’s experience the incumbent bank was the most common loss, followed by no decision, because of relationship and inertia.
- **E2-G12 · line 51** — Maria views bank competition and inertia as more important than competition among fintechs in this segment at present.
- **E2-G13 · line 55** — Maria favors win rate against the incumbent bank over a win rate against fintech competitors.
- **E2-G14 · line 59** — Verify win-rate definitions against the underlying opportunity list; Maria supplies no benchmark win-rate percentage.
- **E2-G15 · line 63** — Maria describes growth through expansion from one distributor division to others, and sees one entity after three years as a warning about post-sale coverage.
- **E2-G16 · line 67** — Maria interprets NRR above 110 as expansion and below 100 as leakage; in the middle she wants the account distribution rather than the average. These are diagnostic thresholds, not Harbor results.
- **E2-G17 · line 71** — Maria flags average rep tenure under eighteen months and geographic rather than vertical territories as possible pipeline and selling-quality risks; Harbor’s actual staffing is unknown.
- **E2-G18 · line 75** — Maria contrasts volume-based incentives with revenue-based incentives and early-churn clawbacks; she argues the former can attract unprofitable accounts.
- **E2-G19 · line 79** — Ask for trigger events on the last twenty closed deals, platform fees by account including zeros, and bank win rates backed by the opportunity list.
- **E2-G20 · line 83** — Maria warns that a poor buyer portal can generate complaints that the distributor attributes to the provider; diligence should include buyer experience.

## Claims the models will check

- **C1 · supported** — Maria describes four to nine months from first call to first transaction for distributors in the twenty-to-two-hundred-million range.
- **C2 · not supported** — Harbor implementations take four to nine months.
- **C3 · supported** — Maria says the CFO signs, but the AR manager decides whether the project starts.
- **C4 · contradicted** — David says middleware cannot work and is a structural failure.
- **C5 · partially supported** — Maria describes a sales cycle of two to three months from first call to first transaction for distributors in the twenty-to-two-hundred-million range.
- **C6 · not supported** — Harbor has net revenue retention above 110 percent.

## Findings that must not be made

- Harbor has measured cash-application savings of four hours to twenty minutes.
- Harbor’s implementation takes four to nine months.
- Harbor has 80% building-products exposure or lost 20% of volume.
- More than half of the market uses checks as an established measurement.
- Harbor has NRR above 110%, a known win rate, or a proven repricing opportunity.
- ERP partners receive a fixed 20% referral fee as a measured or universal fact.
- David ranked purchasing criteria as interchange spread, float, software.
- Maria fully agrees that reconciliation is what gets the first meeting.
- The two experts represent a market survey or observed Harbor customer outcomes.

## Evidence gaps

- No measured Harbor financials, retention, churn or customer outcomes.
- No verified market size, check penetration or market-wide buying behavior.
- No observed Harbor implementation duration or bank win-rate benchmark.
- No Harbor integration inventory, connector volume share, active-use series or partner economics.
- No Harbor customer/vertical concentration, repricing outcomes or sales-compensation data.
- Two fictional expert perspectives cannot establish causality or represent a market survey.

## Review boundary

Approving this key freezes the expected facts and verdicts. Each provider then receives the same canonical sources for extraction, and the same approved gold evidence for controlled synthesis and claim checks. Provider-specific extraction results are reviewed separately with model names hidden; model-passed findings are never silently treated as human-confirmed. The two-interview pilot can identify obvious regressions and costs, but cannot establish general model superiority.
