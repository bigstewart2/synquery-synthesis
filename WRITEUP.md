# Writeup

## Thesis

The interview guide is already a schema. The agent asks typed questions — ranked criteria, 1-10 ratings per vendor per criterion, per-user prices, percentages, satisfaction scores on a stated scale — so synthesis is typed extraction against that schema, with every piece of arithmetic done deterministically afterward and every number traceable to a quoted span and a timestamp. The client's actual job is to put a claim on a slide and defend it in a partner meeting, so the product's job is to say, for each claim, what this panel actually allows them to claim.

## Architecture

AI does the work that requires reading comprehension: locating and typing the atoms in a transcript (a rating, a price, a ranked criterion, a quote that supports a takeaway), and drafting the takeaway prose that sits next to each finding. Everything downstream of that is deterministic and lives in code, not in a model call: every count, every mean, every ratio, the consensus-vs-contested marker on each rating cell, the comparable-vs-not-comparable status of each criterion, scale-mismatch detection, citation link validation, redaction, and the banned-term scan that gates the build. What's stored is atoms — an extracted quote plus a timestamp — and a hand-verified facts file that acts as ground truth for this prototype. What's computed at read time or build time is every aggregate, every evidentiary tag, and every claim verdict on the Summary tab; none of that is hand-typed into the render code. A prior scored extraction run against this same panel came back at 52 of 52 vendor ratings matched, 11 of 11 prices matched, 106 of 106 quotes verbatim against the source transcripts, and zero hallucinated atoms — the schema-first approach held up against ground truth.

## Data-quality findings as requirements on the synthesis layer

None of the following are a grade on the interviewer; each is a requirement the synthesis layer needs to meet, with a cheap fix. A scale mismatch — the agent asks on a 1-7 scale and the expert answers 9 out of 10, and the interview moves on — needs a units validator that quarantines that row from every aggregate rather than averaging it in. Bare acquiescence to a fed hypothesis — the agent supplies a conclusion and the expert only agrees, with nothing added — needs an evidentiary tag that weights confirmed-on-prompt below a volunteered answer, with a middle state for a fed hypothesis the expert confirmed and then added first-hand specifics to. A price basis mismatch — one price paid, one price quoted but never paid, one price set as an illustrative comparison anchor — needs a typed basis field on every price so unlike bases never get averaged together. A redaction inconsistency — the header is anonymized but the body names the employer, and the employer even appears under two different spellings in the same transcript — needs entity resolution at ingest into a single redaction map, not per-string patching. Non-standard criteria per expert, where only two of eight rated criteria were asked of the full panel, is a fix at interview design, not at synthesis: standardize the criteria at setup and the comparison grid fills itself. ASR and agent artifacts — a duplicated wrap-up close, a mis-heard acronym corrected mid-turn — need a transcript QC pass before extraction runs, so the extractor is never asked to make sense of a known glitch.

## Roadmap

The order here is failure order, not ambition order: each item sits where its absence first hurts a real client, and the first item cannot wait past next week.

1. **Reviewer sign-off before anything reaches the client.** A lightweight queue where a human walks every takeaway, every rating cell, and every one of the Summary tab's claim verdicts, approves or edits each one, and the site publishes only what carries a sign-off. Days of work, not weeks. The build's automated checks catch structural failures, a broken citation or a scale mismatch; they cannot catch a judgment call, and one wrong claim in front of a PE partner ends the design partnership in week one. First because it is the only item that must exist before the ship date. It also pays twice: every accept, edit, and reject is labeled data on where extraction fails, which item 5 consumes.
2. **A typed interview guide, confirmed at setup, derived from the client's question.** The client states the question they need answered; the system proposes the goal type it implies and the client confirms or overrides. This project's question was competitive positioning, which sets a vendor-by-criterion matrix and a guide built to fill it: every criterion asked of every expert, on one stated scale, in typed formats the extractor already reads. Only 2 of 8 rated criteria came back comparable across this panel, and that defect was born in the guide and cannot be repaired at synthesis. Second because the guide for the design partner's next project gets written at setup, so a month of delay here costs a full cycle of another mostly empty grid. The broader library of goal types, each with its own matrix shape (a pricing panel keys to price by segment with basis tags, a diligence panel to customer by satisfaction and renewal), should generalize from the first few real projects rather than be invented now from a sample of one. Coverage gaps computed against a goal-derived guide then become targeted question blocks for the next commissioned call.
3. **Real ingestion on an atom store.** Replace three hand-placed text files with a pipeline: finished interviews land keyed to a project, atoms carry stable IDs and provenance (transcript version, source span, extractor versus human), and a corrected transcript or an improved extractor produces a diff against the accepted set, unchanged, changed, new, orphaned, rather than a fresh run that silently discards sign-offs and renumbers citations a client already pasted into a deck. Per-project isolation gets drawn now, while it is cheap, because it becomes the permission boundary the moment a second firm's data arrives. Third because the team hits this daily from week two onward, and the review gate and goal library both need atoms to have a home to write status into. This is the first month's engineering, not the first week's.
4. **Export into the client's actual deck.** One-click export of the ratings matrix, selected findings, and the Summary tab's claim verdicts as native slide and spreadsheet objects with the citation footnotes attached. Today's copy-with-citation means clients retype our numbers, which is exactly where transcription errors enter and where the traceability story quietly dies; the deliverable they defend in a partner meeting is their deck, not our page. Fourth because it turns a page they admire into a tool they renew, but a week of copy-paste is survivable where an unreviewed claim or a wasted project cycle is not.
5. **Extraction eval harness on every project.** Turn the one hand-scored panel (52 of 52 ratings, 106 of 106 verbatim quotes) into a standing accuracy score, built from sampled human spot-checks plus every correction the review queue records, with extraction changes run against that accumulated ground truth before they ship. One perfect panel of three is a demo result, not a warranty, and the review gate only stays cheap if reviewers trust extraction enough to sample instead of re-reading everything. Last not because it matters least but because it needs the project volume items 2 and 3 generate; those projects are its test set.

## Where an engineer is wanted before this carries load

Schema generation from an arbitrary interview guide (rather than a hand-written one per project), an extraction eval harness that runs at volume instead of one hand-scored panel, entity resolution for redaction that generalizes past a small hand-built map, multi-tenant storage and permissions once more than one client's data lives in the same system, and the review-queue workflow itself — routing, sign-off, and versioning once a human is in the loop on every delivery.

## Deliberately left out

No free-text claim checker on the Summary tab — a fixed set of five claims keeps every verdict deterministic and honest at a panel of three; an open input box would invite verdicts this panel cannot actually support. Separately: the Summary tab completes the layer that a prior draft's Answer tab had marked parked, rather than sitting beside it as a second, competing thing — that tab's own text said the work was on hold pending a call, and the call was to build this instead.
